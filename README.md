# Astro Journey

A public log of my transition from frontend architecture into the space and astronomy industry.

## Who I am

I'm Brennan — a Lead Frontend Architect with 9+ years of experience across e-commerce
(Carter's/OshKosh), mission-critical enterprise tooling for emergency services (Cox
Communications), and early-stage startup platform work (Chatterworks). Full-stack
TypeScript, React/Next.js, Node, Postgres/Mongo. Based in Brooklyn.

## Why this repo exists

Astronomy, cosmology, and astrophysics have been a lifelong pull for me, separate from
my career. At 35, I've decided to stop treating them as separate.

The goal: redirect my engineering career toward the space and astronomy industry —
working on tools, missions, and software that actually matter to me — while building
genuine domain knowledge in the field over the long term.

This repo is the public record of that work. It's an accountability mechanism, a
study log, and eventually a portfolio of contributions.

## The plan

**Engineering track (immediate):**
- Contribute to open-source astronomy/space software (astropy, JS9, Aladin Lite)
- Build a substantial personal project at the intersection of web engineering and astronomy
- Network into the space industry — newer space companies, astronomy software orgs,
  observatories — and land a senior/staff engineering role in the field
- Run this in parallel with my current job search; no romantic decisions about income

**Domain track (long arc):**
- Build the math and physics foundations I never got formally (algebra → calculus →
  mechanics → E&M → linear algebra → diff eq)
- Then real astrophysics: Susskind's Theoretical Minimum, Maoz, eventually Carroll & Ostlie
- Active amateur astronomy: AAVSO contributions, observing, joining NYC astronomy community
- Realistic timeline: 12-18 months to be ready for serious astrophysics texts

## Starting point

- May 24, 2026
- Math: self-taught, gaps in fundamentals (limited formal high school math/science)
- Physics: hobbyist-level reading, no formal coursework
- Engineering: senior, 9+ years, no astronomy domain experience yet

## Log

Entries live in `/log`, dated. Short is fine. Showing up is the point.

## Why public

Because the only way I've ever finished anything hard is by being on the hook for it.
And because if any of this helps someone else make a similar move, that's worth the
small embarrassment of being seen learning in public.
